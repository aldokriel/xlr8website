Real Hong Kong Cases

These are real cases involving teenagers and young people in Hong Kong in the last few years. Most started with something that looked small: an ad for quick cash, a vape from a friend, a 'joke' post or a prank video. Each one ended with an arrest.

The pattern to remember: criminals recruit teenagers online because they think teens will work cheaply, won't ask questions and won't be caught. They are wrong about the last part: it's usually the teenager who gets arrested.
Attack June 2025 · sentenced October 2026

Teen jailed 8 years for HK$10,000 acid attack on a tourist

A 17-year-old threw sulphuric acid at a 36-year-old Singaporean tourist outside a building in Wan Chai. She spent 61 days in hospital, needed skin grafts and has permanent scars. He admitted he did it for a HK$10,000 reward arranged through bad company, and the High Court jailed him for eight years.

Lesson: Easy money from criminals always has a price, and it can cost years of your life.

July 2026

Eight students as young as 12 arrested for splashing red paint for a gang

Police arrested eight students, six of them aged 12 to 16, over six red-paint attacks on homes and shops in Tsim Sha Tsui, Yau Ma Tei, Kwun Tong and Tin Shui Wai. They were recruited through social media to work for a triad-backed illegal debt-collection ring and were arrested for criminal damage.

Lesson: 'Quick jobs' advertised online are often crimes, and the young people doing them are the ones who get caught.

August 2026

13- and 14-year-olds used as debt collectors by a HK$200 million loan-shark ring

A triad-linked loan-shark gang recruited students aged 13 and 14 through 'quick cash' ads on Telegram and other apps. The teens threatened borrowers and splashed red paint on their homes. Twenty-five people aged 13 to 69 were arrested, facing charges including criminal damage, criminal intimidation and money laundering.

Lesson: If an ad promises fast cash for 'simple tasks', ask what the task really is.

March 2024

15-year-old girl arrested for collecting cash from scammed elderly people

Fraudsters recruited a 15-year-old girl on Instagram to collect money in 'guess who I am' phone scams. The victims, mostly elderly people living alone, were told their son had been arrested and needed bail; one 81-year-old woman lost HK$1.26 million. The girl was arrested for obtaining property by deception, which carries up to 10 years in jail.

Lesson: 'Just pick up a bag' is never just a pickup. You become part of the scam.

September 2025

17-year-old charged after taking HK$500-a-job offer from scammers

A 17-year-old was lured online with an offer of HK$500 per job and became part of a scam targeting elderly people. Seven victims aged 68 to 86 lost about HK$1.1 million after callers pretended to be their arrested sons or grandsons. He was charged with obtaining property by deception and held in custody.

Lesson: HK$500 is not worth a criminal record or helping rob someone's grandparent.

October 2026

Fake 'model job' ads turned young people's e-wallets into scam accounts

A gang posted fake modelling-job ads on social media and told applicants to set up e-wallets with phone numbers the gang supplied, giving the criminals control. The accounts were used to launder money from fake concert-ticket scams, where nearly 60% of the victims were students. Ten people aged 13 to 68 were arrested, including a university student couple said to be core members.

Lesson: Never set up or hand over a bank account, e-wallet or phone number for anyone else.

2025

15-year-old arrested at home after a classmate brought a 'space oil' vape

A 15-year-old classmate brought an e-cigarette containing etomidate ('space oil') to a girl's home in Tai Po and persuaded her to try it. Her father called the police and she was arrested for possessing dangerous drugs; the classmate was wanted by police. Etomidate has been a dangerous drug in Hong Kong since February 2025 and can cause addiction, seizures and death.

Lesson: A vape from a friend can be a dangerous drug, and having it is a crime.

July 2026

17-year-old barista and university students arrested at HK$3 million drug lab

Police raided a flat in Cheung Sha Wan and found about 1kg of etomidate powder, 300 finished vape cartridges, ketamine and over 10,000 empty cartridges. Four people aged 17 to 21 were arrested: a 17-year-old barista and three university students.

Lesson: 'Space oil' is made in illegal labs by people who don't care who gets hurt.

July 2026

DSE student charged over online bomb 'joke'

A 19-year-old DSE candidate posted on social media that explosives had been planted at an exam centre in Kwai Chung. Police searched the site, found nothing, and arrested her the next day; she told police it was a joke. She was charged with passing on false bomb information, which carries up to five years in jail and a HK$150,000 fine.

Lesson: Police treat threats as real. A 'joke' post can lead to criminal charges.

February 2024

Six teens arrested after 'prank' video on street sleepers went viral

Six teenagers aged 15 to 17 were filmed splashing water and Coke on a homeless man in a pedestrian tunnel, and the video spread on social media. Police linked them to similar incidents in Happy Valley and Wan Chai and arrested them for disorder in a public place.

Lesson: Filming a 'prank' gives police the evidence. Cruelty for views can get you arrested.

April 2026

Young woman arrested for doxxing an old schoolmate after a falling-out

After a fight with a former secondary schoolmate, a 23-year-old posted the victim's photos, workplace, part of her ID card and a medical report on social media. Hong Kong's Privacy Commissioner arrested her for disclosing personal data without consent.

Lesson: Anger is never a reason to post someone's personal information. Doxxing is a crime.
